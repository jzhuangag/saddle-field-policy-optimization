\begin{abstract}
Two-player zero-sum Markov games model robust reinforcement learning and competitive policy optimization.
Rotational components in their saddle fields can hinder first-order updates.
We identify the policy field $\bF(\bz)$ and a Jacobian-induced curvature direction $\bG(\bz)=\nabla\bF(\bz)\bF(\bz)$ for rotational coupling.
A symmetric--antisymmetric Jacobian decomposition characterizes when the curvature direction decreases field energy.
We then develop a geometry-aware controller that assigns separate step sizes to the two directions.
Minimizing a local model of the one-step change in a composite Lyapunov function $\Vk$ yields the two step sizes through a box-constrained quadratic program (QP).
Under local geometric coverage, the controller attains an $O(1/K)$ bound over $K$ joint updates on the time-average expected stationarity residual, plus an explicit residual floor.
A positive-semidefinite symmetric part and nonsingularity of the metric-transformed Jacobian at the target stationary point are shown to imply neighborhood coverage, including the nonsingular pure-rotation case.
Under a local policy-space error bound, this stationarity result transfers to guarantees on the unregularized Nash gap and the maximizing player's worst-case-return loss.
Experiments across four settings validate the predicted geometry; at the final checkpoint of every Markov-game task, the controller has higher mean worst-case return and lower mean exploitability than the matched field-only ablation, including a $79.1\%$ exploitability reduction on neural CyclicControl.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================
\IEEEPARstart{T}{wo-player} zero-sum Markov games provide a mathematical model for competitive sequential decisions \cite{Shapley1953StochasticGames,Littman1994MarkovGames,Alacaoglu2022NACMG}.
They also underpin robust reinforcement learning (RL), where a maximizing policy must retain performance against model uncertainty, disturbances, or adversarial actions represented by a minimizing policy \cite{SuttonBarto2018,MorimotoDoya2005RobustRL,Iyengar2005RobustDP,NilimElGhaoui2005,Pinto2017RARL,Kamalaruban2020LangevinRARL}.

Policy parameterization yields joint ascent--descent dynamics estimated from stochastic trajectories.
Unlike an ordinary minimization gradient, the resulting saddle field may contain a strong rotational component that makes gradient descent--ascent (GDA) cycle or diverge even on bilinear games; related instability arises in robust-control formulations of adversarial RL \cite{ZhangHuBasar2020StabilityRARL}.

The proximal point method (PPM) and extragradient-type methods, including the classical extragradient method (EGM), Mirror-Prox, and policy extragradient, introduce corrections that can suppress rotational dynamics at suitable step sizes \cite{Rockafellar1976PPM,Korpelevich1976Extragradient,Nemirovski2004MirrorProx,Mokhtari2020UnifiedEG,CenWeiChi2024FastPolicyEG}.
Recent analyses of saddle algorithms relate these corrections to Jacobian-induced dynamics in the underlying game field \cite{Lu2022ODE,Balduzzi2018Mechanics,Letcher2019DifferentiableGameMechanics,Mescheder2018GANConvergence,Loizou2020SHGD}.
Adaptive variational-inequality and extra-gradient methods adjust the extrapolation and update scales from observed operator information \cite{Hsieh2020DSEG,Antonakopoulos2021AdaProx}.
Our controller instead treats the field and Jacobian-induced curvature directions as separate search coordinates and selects their step sizes by minimizing a local model of composite Lyapunov change.

We represent the parameterized game as a saddle field with two complementary update directions.
The saddle field $\bF(\bz)$ defines the joint first-order update direction $-\bF(\bz)$, while the curvature direction $+\bG(\bz)=\nabla\bF(\bz)\bF(\bz)$ captures the local effect of rotational coupling.
An exact symmetric--antisymmetric Jacobian condition shows that $+\bG$ decreases the squared field norm precisely when the antisymmetric action dominates the symmetric action along $\bF$.

Because the effective balance also changes along the trajectory and under stochastic noise, we extend Lyapunov-drift step-size design to select both magnitudes online using their local geometry \cite{Zhang2025AdaptivePolyak,Yuan2025StepSizeSDE}.

The resulting controller minimizes a local second-order model of a composite Lyapunov function through a two-dimensional box-constrained quadratic program (QP), yielding closed-form step sizes, finite-time stationarity guarantees, and policy-space performance bounds.

The main contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{Curvature geometry.}
We derive an exact symmetric--antisymmetric Jacobian criterion for curvature-induced field-energy descent and connect it to the correction shared by PPM and EGM but absent from GDA.
\item \textbf{Adaptive Lyapunov-drift control.}
A composite merit combining weighted field energy and a regularized policy-space gap yields separate field and curvature step sizes through a closed-form box QP.
Its local model dominates every feasible field-only, curvature-only, or fixed-coupling comparator in the same box, with strict interior improvement characterized by a reduced gradient.
\item \textbf{Finite-time and policy-space guarantees.}
For biased stochastic oracles, two-direction geometric coverage yields an $O(1/K)$ transient in the time-average expected stationarity residual plus an explicit error floor.
We derive neighborhood coverage from a positive-semidefinite symmetric part and nonsingularity of the metric-transformed Jacobian at the target, including pure rotation; under a local policy-space error bound, the stationarity estimate transfers to ergodic Nash-gap and worst-case-return bounds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Saddle-Field Dynamics in Zero-Sum Markov Games}\label{sec:prelim}

% ============================================================

\subsection{Zero-Sum Markov Game and Saddle-Field Formulation}\label{subsec:formulation}

We consider an infinite-horizon discounted two-player zero-sum Markov game with state space $\mathcal{S}$, maximizing-player action space $\mathcal{A}$, minimizing-player action space $\mathcal{B}$, transition kernel $P$, bounded reward $r$ to the maximizing player, discount factor $\alpha\in(0,1)$, and initial-state distribution $\rho$.
For a maximizing-player policy $\pi$ and a minimizing-player policy $\nu$, the maximizing player's discounted return is
\[
J(\pi,\nu)
\triangleq
\E_{\rho,\pi,\nu}
\!\left[
\textstyle\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{t}r(s_{t},a_{t},b_{t})
\right].
\]
Here $\E_{\rho,\pi,\nu}$ denotes expectation under the initial-state distribution and the two policies.
The policy-space saddle objective is $\sup_{\pi}\inf_{\nu} J(\pi,\nu)$.
When $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ are finite, define the discounted causal entropies
\begin{align*}
\mathsf H_{\alpha}^{\pi}(\pi,\nu)
&\triangleq \E_{\rho,\pi,\nu}\!\left[\sum_{t\ge0}\alpha^t
\bigl(-\log\pi(a_t\mid s_t)\bigr)\right],\\
\mathsf H_{\alpha}^{\nu}(\pi,\nu)
&\triangleq \E_{\rho,\pi,\nu}\!\left[\sum_{t\ge0}\alpha^t
\bigl(-\log\nu(b_t\mid s_t)\bigr)\right],
\end{align*}
with $0\log0=0$.
For entropy weight $\varepsilon\ge0$, the corresponding regularized return is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropic_return}
J_{\varepsilon}(\pi,\nu)
\triangleq J(\pi,\nu)
+\varepsilon\mathsf H_{\alpha}^{\pi}(\pi,\nu)
-\varepsilon\mathsf H_{\alpha}^{\nu}(\pi,\nu).
\end{equation}
The opposite entropy signs follow the saddle convention: the maximizing player raises $J_\varepsilon$ by increasing its entropy, while the minimizing player lowers $J_\varepsilon$ by increasing its own entropy.
Thus, entropy encourages stochastic responses for both players and replaces potentially set-valued hard best responses by soft best responses in finite games.
Without regularization, the supremum or infimum defining a best-response value can kink when its optimizer switches, invalidating the gradient--Hessian quadratic model used by the controller.
For $\varepsilon>0$, this smoothing supplies the differentiable policy-space gap needed by the first- and second-order Lyapunov model; Proposition~\ref{prop:smooth_entropy_gap} formalizes the required local regularity.
Because regularization perturbs the original performance criterion, Proposition~\ref{prop:performance_bridge} later transfers the regularized guarantee to the unregularized Nash gap and worst-case return with an explicit entropy-bias term.
Let $J_{\rm alg}\in\{J,J_\varepsilon\}$ denote the differentiable saddle objective.
This notation separates the objective differentiated by the controller from the performance measure used for final evaluation.
The finite-game controller and all Markov-game experiments use $J_{\rm alg}=J_\varepsilon$, while Section~\ref{subsec:performance_guarantee} reserves $J$ for unregularized performance measures.
In large finite or continuous state spaces, we adopt the standard parameterized formulation because exact policy-space optimization is generally intractable.
The maximizing-player policy is represented by $\pi_{\btheta}(a\mid s)$ with $\btheta\in\R^{d_{\theta}}$, and the minimizing-player policy by $\nu_{\bpsi}(b\mid s)$ with $\bpsi\in\R^{d_{\psi}}$, giving
\[
\begin{aligned}
J_{\rm alg}(\btheta,\bpsi)
&\triangleq
J_{\rm alg}(\pi_{\btheta},\nu_{\bpsi}),\\
&\hspace{3em}
\max_{\btheta}\min_{\bpsi}J_{\rm alg}(\btheta,\bpsi).
\end{aligned}
\]
Write $\bz\triangleq(\btheta,\bpsi)\in\R^d$, where $d\triangleq d_\theta+d_\psi$.
The joint policy ascent--descent dynamics define the saddle field
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fdef}
\bF(\bz)
\triangleq
\begin{bmatrix}
-\nabla_{\btheta}J_{\rm alg}(\btheta,\bpsi)\\[0.2em]
\phantom{-}\nabla_{\bpsi}J_{\rm alg}(\btheta,\bpsi)
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The first-order stationary set is $\mathcal Z^{\star}\triangleq\{\bz:\bF(\bz)=\bzero\}$, where $\bzero$ is the zero vector.
Every $\bz^\star\in\mathcal Z^\star$ satisfies the unconstrained first-order Karush--Kuhn--Tucker (KKT) conditions for the parameterized zero-sum objective.
Under neural policy parameterization, $\mathcal Z^\star$ is the parameter-space first-order stationary set.
This distinction matters because a nonconvex or noninjective policy parameterization can satisfy $\bF(\bz)=\bzero$ even when a player can still improve by taking a best response in the full policy space.
The policy-space results in Section~\ref{subsec:performance_guarantee} use the Nash gap to measure this remaining unilateral-improvement incentive and additionally bound the maximizing player's worst-case-return loss.

\subsection{Curvature-Induced Dynamics and Jacobian Geometry}\label{subsec:saddle_field}

The saddle-field formulation exposes the rotational component that can destabilize first-order updates.
To understand how established saddle methods counter this effect, we examine the Jacobian-based correction generated by PPM and EGM.
For a scalar step size $s>0$, GDA, PPM, and EGM use the representative updates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classical_updates}
\begin{aligned}
\textnormal{GDA:}&\quad
\bz_{+}^{\rm GDA}=\bz-s\bF(\bz),\\
\textnormal{PPM:}&\quad
\bz_{+}^{\rm PPM}=\bz-s\bF(\bz_{+}^{\rm PPM}),\\
\textnormal{EGM:}&\quad
\widetilde{\bz}=\bz-s\bF(\bz),\quad
\bz_{+}^{\rm EGM}=\bz-s\bF(\widetilde{\bz}),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde{\bz}$ is the EGM extrapolation point.
GDA is explicit, PPM is implicit, and EGM uses an extrapolated field query.
Below, $\norm{\cdot}$ denotes the Euclidean norm and its induced operator and tensor norms, $\inner{\cdot}{\cdot}$ the standard inner product, $\nabla\bF$ the Jacobian of $\bF$ with respect to $\bz$, and $\nabla^2\bF$ its second-derivative tensor.
We write $\Id_n$ for the $n\times n$ identity matrix and $\Id$ when its dimension is clear; for a square matrix $\boldsymbol{X}$, $\sym(\boldsymbol{X})\triangleq(\boldsymbol{X}+\boldsymbol{X}^\top)/2$ and $\skewop(\boldsymbol{X})\triangleq(\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{X}^\top)/2$, while $\boldsymbol{X}\succeq0$ and $\boldsymbol{X}\succ0$ respectively denote positive semidefiniteness and positive definiteness when $\boldsymbol{X}$ is symmetric.

The following assumption states the local regularity required by the template expansion and one-step analysis.

\begin{assumption}[Local smoothness]\label{ass:smooth}
There exists an open set $\cD\subset\R^{d}$ such that:
(a) all iterates and probing points used in the analysis lie in $\cD$;
(b) $J_{\rm alg}:\R^{d}\to\R$ is four times continuously differentiable ($C^{4}$) on $\cD$;
(c) all derivatives of $J_{\rm alg}$ up to order four are bounded on $\cD$.
\end{assumption}

Conditions~(b)--(c) imply that $\bF$ is three times continuously differentiable, i.e. $\bF\in C^{3}(\cD;\R^{d})$; after prescribing an implementable region $\mathcal T\subset\cD$, the safeguards of Section~\ref{sec:impl} can maintain Condition~(a).

\begin{proposition}[Discrete-time second-order template expansions]\label{prop:DTA}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:smooth} holds and fix $\bz\in\cD$.
There exists $\bar s>0$ such that, for every $s\in(0,\bar s]$, the PPM equation has a unique local solution, the EGM extrapolation and update points lie in $\cD$, and
\begin{align*}
\textnormal{GDA:}\quad \bz_{+}-\bz&=-s\bF(\bz),\\
\textnormal{PPM:}\quad \bz_{+}-\bz&=-s\bF(\bz)+s^{2}\nabla\bF(\bz)\bF(\bz)+R^{\mathrm{PPM}}_{s}(\bz),\\
\textnormal{EGM:}\quad \bz_{+}-\bz&=-s\bF(\bz)+s^{2}\nabla\bF(\bz)\bF(\bz)+R^{\mathrm{EGM}}_{s}(\bz),
\end{align*}
with $\norm{R^{\mathrm{PPM}}_{s}(\bz)}\le C_{\mathrm{PPM}}s^{3}$ and $\norm{R^{\mathrm{EGM}}_{s}(\bz)}\le C_{\mathrm{EGM}}s^{3}$ for finite constants depending only on local bounds of $\bF$, $\nabla\bF$, and $\nabla^{2}\bF$ on $\cD$.
\end{proposition}

Unlike GDA, PPM and EGM share the leading-order correction $+s^{2}\nabla\bF(\bz)\bF(\bz)$, which can suppress rotational saddle dynamics at suitable step sizes.
This contrast motivates writing $\bA(\bz)\triangleq\nabla\bF(\bz)$ and isolating the curvature-induced direction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curvature_direction}
\bG(\bz)\triangleq\bA(\bz)\bF(\bz).
\end{equation}
For any fixed symmetric positive-definite metric $\bM$, define the metric field energy $\cE_{\bM}(\bz)\triangleq\tfrac12\bF(\bz)^\top\bM\bF(\bz)$ and its exact-expectation (population) decrease signals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geometry_signals}
\begin{aligned}
\chi_F^{\bM}(\bz)
&\triangleq-\inner{\nabla\cE_{\bM}(\bz)}{-\bF(\bz)}
=\bF^\top\sym(\bM\bA)\bF,\\
\chi_G^{\bM}(\bz)
&\triangleq-\inner{\nabla\cE_{\bM}(\bz)}{\bG(\bz)}
=-\bF^\top\bM\bA^2\bF.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Positive values certify first-order field-energy decrease along the corresponding directions.
The Euclidean choice $\bM=\Id$ gives $\phi(\bz)\triangleq\cE_{\Id}(\bz)=\tfrac12\norm{\bF(\bz)}^{2}$, which is nonnegative and vanishes exactly on $\mathcal Z^\star$.

We first illustrate this geometric criterion on the bilinear max--min saddle $L(x,y)=-xy$, which has field $\bF=J_{0}\bz$ with
\[
J_{0}
\triangleq
\begin{bmatrix}
0&1\\
-1&0
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
We denote the nonnegative step sizes along $-\bF$ and $+\bG$ by $\beta$ and $\gamma$, respectively.
Fig.~\ref{fig:motivation_geometry} uses this bilinear saddle to connect the behavior of the classical updates with the separate control of the field and curvature directions.
Panel (a) compares the update trajectories: GDA has eigenvalue modulus $\sqrt{1+s^{2}}>1$ and spirals outward, PPM contracts for every $s>0$, and EGM contracts for $0<s<1$ (is neutral at $s=1$ and expansive for $s>1$).
Panel (b) explains this contrast geometrically.
Since $J_{0}^{2}=-\Id_{2}$, $\bG=J_0^2\bz=-\bz=-\nabla\phi$ points inward, whereas $-\bF$ is tangent to the field-energy level sets; hence, the shared $+s^{2}\bG$ correction enables PPM and EGM to counter the rotational behavior that destabilizes GDA in this example.
Panel (c) compares four field--curvature step-size combinations.
Proposition~\ref{prop:DTA} and Eq.~\eqref{eq:curvature_direction} show that PPM and EGM couple these step sizes as $(s,s^2)$ to second order, whereas the distinct trajectories in panel (c) motivate selecting $\beta$ and $\gamma$ independently.

To characterize when $+\bG$ decreases the Euclidean field energy, fix $\bz\in\cD$, write $\bA=\bA(\bz)$, and split it into its symmetric and antisymmetric parts
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bA&=\bS+\bW,\quad \bS=\sym(\bA),\quad \bW=\skewop(\bA).
\end{aligned}
\]
The symmetric part $\bS$ captures nonrotational dynamics, whereas the antisymmetric part $\bW$ captures rotational dynamics \cite{Balduzzi2018Mechanics}.
The following lemma expresses the field-energy directional derivatives exactly in terms of $\bS$ and $\bW$.

\begin{lemma}[Symmetric--antisymmetric decomposition of the field-energy derivatives]\label{lem:decomp}
Let $\phi(\bz)=\tfrac12\norm{\bF(\bz)}^{2}$ and $\bA=\nabla\bF(\bz)$, so that $\nabla\phi(\bz)=\bA^{\top}\bF(\bz)$.
The directional derivatives of $\phi$ along $-\bF$ and $+\bG=\bA\bF$ satisfy
\begin{align}
\inner{\nabla\phi(\bz)}{-\bF(\bz)}
&=-\bF(\bz)^{\top}\bS\,\bF(\bz),
\label{eq:dphi_F}\\
\inner{\nabla\phi(\bz)}{\bG(\bz)}
&=\norm{\bS\bF(\bz)}^{2}-\norm{\bW\bF(\bz)}^{2}.
\label{eq:dphi_G}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

The compact form in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dphi_G} uses the cancellation $\bF^{\top}(\bS\bW+\bW\bS)\bF=0$, since $\bS\bW+\bW\bS$ is antisymmetric.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dphi_F} shows that the field direction decreases field energy through the symmetric part: in a purely rotational field ($\bS=0$), $-\bF$ is tangent to the level sets of $\phi$, exactly the GDA cycling mechanism.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dphi_G} balances the symmetric action $\norm{\bS\bF}^{2}$ against the antisymmetric action $\norm{\bW\bF}^{2}$ and yields the following skew-dominance criterion.

\begin{theorem}[Skew-dominance characterization of curvature descent]\label{thm:skew_dominance}
Fix $\bz\in\cD$ with $\bF(\bz)\neq\bzero$, and define $\bA=\nabla\bF(\bz)$, $\bS=\sym(\bA)$, and $\bW=\skewop(\bA)$.
The curvature direction $+\bG(\bz)$ is a strict descent direction for the field energy $\phi$ at $\bz$, i.e. $\inner{\nabla\phi(\bz)}{\bG(\bz)}<0$, if and only if the antisymmetric action dominates the symmetric action along $\bF$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:skew_dominance}
\norm{\bW\bF(\bz)}
>
\norm{\bS\bF(\bz)}.
\end{equation}
In particular:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item if the field is purely rotational at $\bz$ ($\bS\bF(\bz)=\bzero$ and $\bW\bF(\bz)\neq\bzero$), then $\inner{\nabla\phi}{-\bF}=0$ and $\inner{\nabla\phi}{\bG}=-\norm{\bW\bF(\bz)}^{2}<0$; hence $+\bG$ strictly decreases $\phi$, whereas $-\bF$ is tangential and has zero first-order effect;
\item if the field is symmetric at $\bz$ ($\bW=\bzero$), then $\inner{\nabla\phi}{\bG}=\norm{\bS\bF(\bz)}^{2}\ge0$; hence $+\bG$ preserves or increases $\phi$ to first order;
\item if $\bA$ is normal ($\bA^\top\bA=\bA\bA^\top$) and its restriction to a two-dimensional real invariant subspace $\mathcal U$ has the orthonormal representation $\bA|_{\mathcal U}=\mu\Id_2+\sigma J_0$, with conjugate eigenvalues $\mu\pm\mathrm{i}\sigma$ ($\mathrm{i}^2=-1$) and $\bF(\bz)\in\mathcal U$, then the condition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:skew_dominance} reduces to $\lvert\sigma\rvert>\lvert\mu\rvert$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

This theorem provides the geometric criterion that motivates the two-direction controller developed in Section~\ref{sec:drift}.
In part~(iii), $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are the real and imaginary parts of the conjugate eigenvalues: for a local coordinate $\bu\in\mathcal U$, the linearized flow $\dot{\bu}=-(\mu\Id_2+\sigma J_0)\bu$ shows that they control radial scaling and oriented angular motion, respectively.
Moreover, $\bS|_{\mathcal U}=\mu\Id_2$ and $\bW|_{\mathcal U}=\sigma J_0$, so $\norm{\bS\bF}=\lvert\mu\rvert\norm{\bF}$ and $\norm{\bW\bF}=\lvert\sigma\rvert\norm{\bF}$ on $\mathcal U$.
Part~(iii) is an interpretable normal-block specialization, not a structural assumption imposed on the general saddle field or on the controller.
Jacobian--vector and Jacobian-transpose--vector products evaluate this condition and yield the measures $\norm{\bW\bF}$ and $\norm{\bS\bF}$ used in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

For the local linear field $\bF(\bz)=\bA(\bz-\bz^\star)$ at $\bF(\bz)\neq\bzero$, $\bG=\bA\bF$ is collinear with $\bF$ exactly when $\bF$ is a real eigenvector of $\bA$.
If also $\bA^\top=-\bA$, $\bA^2=-\sigma^2\Id$, and $\sigma\neq0$, then for $\bu=\bz-\bz^\star\neq\bzero$, $\bF=\bA\bu$ is tangential and $\bG=-\sigma^2\bu$ is orthogonally inward, with $\inner{\nabla\phi}{-\bF}=0$ and $\inner{\nabla\phi}{\bG}=-\sigma^4\norm{\bu}^2<0$.

\subsection{From Classical Templates to a Stochastic Recursion}\label{subsec:oracle_model}
Our aim is a simultaneous stochastic policy update for zero-sum Markov games that adapts to rotational coupling.
Relative to GDA's step $-s\bF$, Proposition~\ref{prop:DTA} shows that PPM and EGM add $+s^2\bG+O(s^3)$; in the rotation-dominated regimes characterized above, this curvature action suppresses the rotational behavior of the field step.
Accordingly, at joint update $k$, let $\hatF_k$ and $\hatG_k$ denote the observed field and curvature directions and let $\beta_k,\gamma_k\ge0$ denote their step sizes; the stochastic two-direction recursion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:update}
\bz_{k+1}
=
\bz_{k}-\beta_{k}\hatF_{k}+\gamma_{k}\hatG_{k},
\end{equation}
With exact population directions, $(s,0)$ gives GDA and $(s,s^2)$ matches PPM/EGM to second order; finite-trajectory policy optimization instead uses stochastic observations \cite{Sutton2000PolicyGradient}.
For the stochastic analysis, let $\{\mathscr F_k\}_{k\ge0}$ be the filtration generated by the algorithmic history revealed before the queries at joint update $k$.

\begin{assumption}[Biased stochastic oracles]\label{ass:oracles}
For every $k$, let $\biasF_k,\biasG_k$ be $\mathscr F_k$-measurable oracle biases and let $\noiseF_k,\noiseG_k$ be conditionally zero-mean noise variables, i.e. $\E[\noiseF_k\mid\mathscr F_k]=\E[\noiseG_k\mid\mathscr F_k]=\bzero$.
The observed directions decompose as
\[
\hatF_{k}
=
\bF(\bz_{k})+\biasF_{k}+\noiseF_{k},
\quad
\hatG_{k}
=
\bG(\bz_{k})+\biasG_{k}+\noiseG_{k},
\]
There exist finite $\mathscr{F}_{k}$-measurable scalars $\bar b^{F}_{k},\bar b^{G}_{k},\sigma_{2,k},\sigma_{3,k}\ge 0$ such that, almost surely,
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item $\norm{\biasF_{k}}\le \bar b^{F}_{k}$ and $\norm{\biasG_{k}}\le \bar b^{G}_{k}$;
\item $\E[\norm{\noiseF_{k}}^{2}+\norm{\noiseG_{k}}^{2} \mid\mathscr{F}_{k}]\le \sigma_{2,k}^{2}$;
\item $\E[\norm{\hatF_{k}}^{3}+\norm{\hatG_{k}}^{3} \mid\mathscr{F}_{k}]\le \sigma_{3,k}^{3}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

Here $\bar b_k^F,\bar b_k^G$ are bias bounds, while $\sigma_{2,k},\sigma_{3,k}$ bound the conditional second noise moment and third oracle moment.
This model covers unbiased mini-batch estimators and bounded bias from truncated trajectories, clipped surrogates, or approximate curvature actions.

\section{Geometry-Aware Adaptive Lyapunov-Drift Control}\label{sec:drift}
% ============================================================
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} shows that the field and curvature directions have different local effects on field energy.
We now encode stationarity and policy performance in a nonnegative Lyapunov function.
This extension is necessary because field energy alone cannot reveal whether a locally stabilizing parameter update improves policy-space performance.
We then distinguish its realized one-step change from its conditional Lyapunov drift and construct an implementable local model for selecting the two step sizes.

\subsection{Lyapunov Merit Function}\label{subsec:V_drift}
The field energy vanishes with the parameter-space stationarity residual, but it does not by itself rule out a parameterization-induced stationary point with positive policy-space exploitability.
Conversely, the Nash gap measures unilateral policy improvement but neither directly measures the local saddle-field residual nor naturally shares its numerical scale.
The following merit function therefore combines the two quantities: field energy guides local optimization, while the regularized gap retains policy-space performance in the step-size decision.

\begin{definition}[Composite Lyapunov function]\label{def:Lyap}
Let $\diag(\cdot)$ denote block-diagonal concatenation and let $\eta_{\psi}>0$ be the relative weight of the minimizing-player gradient block.
Define the block-diagonal weight matrix
\[
\bW_{V}\triangleq\diag(\Id_{d_{\theta}},\,\eta_{\psi}\Id_{d_{\psi}})\in\R^{d\times d},
\]
The weighted field-energy component is $\cE_{F}(\bz)\triangleq\cE_{\bW_V}(\bz)=\tfrac12\bF(\bz)^{\top}\bW_{V}\bF(\bz)$.
Henceforth, $\chi_F\triangleq\chi_F^{\bW_V}$ and $\chi_G\triangleq\chi_G^{\bW_V}$ denote the corresponding population decrease signals already defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:geometry_signals}.
For finite games, fix $\varepsilon>0$, write $J_\varepsilon=J_\varepsilon(\pi_{\btheta},\nu_{\bpsi})$, and define the performance component
\[
\begin{aligned}
\cP(\bz)
&\triangleq\operatorname{Gap}_{\varepsilon}(\pi_{\btheta},\nu_{\bpsi})\\
&=\left[\sup_{\bar\pi}J_{\varepsilon}(\bar\pi,\nu_{\bpsi})-J_\varepsilon\right]
+\left[J_\varepsilon-\inf_{\bar\nu}J_{\varepsilon}(\pi_{\btheta},\bar\nu)\right],
\end{aligned}
\]
The extrema range over stationary randomized policies; Eq.~\eqref{eq:entropic_return} defines $J_\varepsilon$.
The first bracket is the return improvement available to the maximizing player through a regularized best response, and the second is the return reduction available to the minimizing player, which seeks a smaller $J_\varepsilon$.
Both gains are nonnegative, so $\cP(\bz)\ge0$, and $\cP(\bz)=0$ exactly at a regularized policy-space Nash equilibrium, where neither player can improve unilaterally.
When this finite-game component is unavailable, taking $\cP\equiv0$ and $\lambda_P=0$ recovers field-energy-only control.
For fixed normalizers $\kappa_{F},\kappa_{P}>0$ and weights $\lambda_{F}>0$, $\lambda_{P}\ge0$, define the composite Lyapunov function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Vdef}
\Vk(\bz)
\triangleq
\lambda_{F}\frac{\cE_{F}(\bz)}{\kappa_{F}}
+
\lambda_{P}\frac{\cP(\bz)}{\kappa_{P}}.
\end{equation}
The normalizers place components with different numerical scales on a comparable basis, while the $\lambda$ weights specify their relative influence on the controller.
\end{definition}

For the recursion in Eq.~\eqref{eq:update}, define the realized one-step Lyapunov increment by $\mathscr I_k\triangleq\Vk(\bz_{k+1})-\Vk(\bz_k)$.
Its conditional expectation
\[
\mathscr D_k\triangleq\E[\mathscr I_k\mid\mathscr F_k]
\]
is the Lyapunov drift at update $k$.
The increment $\mathscr I_k$ is pathwise after the current stochastic queries are realized, whereas $\mathscr D_k$ averages this increment conditional on the pre-query history $\mathscr F_k$.
Equivalently, $\mathscr D_k<0$ means that the next update decreases the composite merit $\Vk$ in conditional expectation; a more negative drift represents a larger expected one-step reduction in the combined stationarity and policy-performance measure.
This interpretation makes minimizing $\mathscr D_k$ the ideal step-size design objective.

The block weight $\bW_V$ is unrelated to the antisymmetric Jacobian part $\bW=\skewop(\nabla\bF)$.

\begin{remark}[From field-energy geometry to Lyapunov control]\label{rem:geometry_merit}
Theorem~\ref{thm:skew_dominance} analyzes $+\bG$ for the field energy $\phi$; Definition~\ref{def:Lyap} extends the same geometry to $\Vk$, whose local drift model determines how strongly curvature is combined with policy performance.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Properties of the composite Lyapunov function]\label{lem:Lyap_properties}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:smooth} holds and $\cP$ is nonnegative and $C^{3}$ on $\cD$.
Then:
(i) $\Vk(\bz)\ge 0$ on $\cD$, and since $\lambda_{F}>0$, $\Vk(\bz)=0$ implies $\bz\in\mathcal{Z}^{\star}$;
(ii) $\Vk\in C^{3}(\cD)$;
(iii) the gradient is $\nabla\Vk=(\lambda_F/\kappa_F)\nabla\cE_F +(\lambda_P/\kappa_P)\nabla\cP$, where $\nabla\cE_F=\nabla\bF^{\top}\bW_V\bF$;
(iv) $\Phi(\bz)\triangleq\bF(\bz)^{\top}\bW_{V}\bF(\bz)$ is the weighted stationarity residual and satisfies $\Phi(\bz)\le2\kappa_{F}\Vk(\bz)/\lambda_{F}$.
\end{lemma}

The composite Lyapunov decrease signals along the two population directions are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:composite_signals}
\begin{aligned}
D_F(\bz)
&\triangleq\inner{\nabla\Vk(\bz)}{\bF(\bz)}
\\[-2pt]
&=\frac{\lambda_F}{\kappa_F}\chi_F(\bz)
+\frac{\lambda_P}{\kappa_P}\inner{\nabla\cP(\bz)}{\bF(\bz)},\\
D_G(\bz)
&\triangleq-\inner{\nabla\Vk(\bz)}{\bG(\bz)}
\\[-2pt]
&=\frac{\lambda_F}{\kappa_F}\chi_G(\bz)
-\frac{\lambda_P}{\kappa_P}\inner{\nabla\cP(\bz)}{\bG(\bz)}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus, $D_F>0$ and $D_G>0$ mean first-order decrease of $\Vk$ along $-\bF$ and $+\bG$, respectively.

\subsection{Second-Order Surrogate for the One-Step Change}\label{subsec:two_direction}
For a realized oracle pair and candidate step sizes $(\beta,\gamma)$, define the trial displacement $\Delta_{k}(\beta,\gamma)\triangleq -\beta\hatF_{k}+\gamma\hatG_{k}$ and the pathwise trial increment $\mathscr I_k(\beta,\gamma)\triangleq\Vk(\bz_k+\Delta_k(\beta,\gamma))-\Vk(\bz_k)$.
The actual quantities satisfy $\mathscr I_k=\mathscr I_k(\beta_k,\gamma_k)$ and $\mathscr D_k=\E[\mathscr I_k(\beta_k,\gamma_k)\mid\mathscr F_k]$.
Constructing a query-dependent rule that exactly minimizes $\mathscr D_k$ would require the full conditional oracle distribution and repeated evaluation of the nonlinear post-update function $\Vk(\bz_k+\Delta_k)$, neither of which is available from a single query.
We therefore minimize, after observing $(\hatF_k,\hatG_k)$, a second-order pathwise surrogate of $\mathscr I_k(\beta,\gamma)$.
The surrogate exposes the separate field curvature, curvature-direction curvature, and cross-curvature needed to select $(\beta_k,\gamma_k)$, while Section~\ref{sec:conv} bounds its mismatch from the realized increment and converts the pathwise choice into an expected finite-time guarantee.

\begin{assumption}[Local regularity along the iterates]\label{ass:local_reg}
For every $k$ and every feasible $(\beta,\gamma)$ produced by the step-size routine, the segment $\{\bz_{k}+t\Delta_{k}(\beta,\gamma):t\in[0,1]\}\subset\cD$.
There exist $\mathscr{F}_{k}$-measurable $G_{V,k},H_{V,k},M_{3,k}\ge 0$ that, simultaneously for every feasible pair and almost every realization of the current stochastic queries, satisfy $\norm{\nabla\Vk(\bz_{k})}\le G_{V,k}$, $\norm{\nabla^{2}\Vk(\bz_{k})}\le H_{V,k}$, and $\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\norm{\nabla^{3}\Vk(\bz_{k}+t\Delta_{k}(\beta,\gamma))} \le M_{3,k}$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Second-order expansion of the one-step change in $\Vk$]\label{lem:drift_taylor}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:local_reg}, for every feasible $(\beta,\gamma)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift_taylor}
\begin{aligned}
&\Vk(\bz_{k}+\Delta_{k}(\beta,\gamma))-\Vk(\bz_{k}) \\
={}&-\beta\inner{\nabla\Vk(\bz_k)}{\hatF_k}
+\gamma\inner{\nabla\Vk(\bz_k)}{\hatG_k}\\
&+\tfrac12\beta^2\hatF_k^\top\nabla^2\Vk(\bz_k)\hatF_k
-\beta\gamma\hatF_k^\top\nabla^2\Vk(\bz_k)\hatG_k\\
&+\tfrac12\gamma^2\hatG_k^\top\nabla^2\Vk(\bz_k)\hatG_k
+\mathrm{rem}_{k}(\beta,\gamma),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\lvert \mathrm{rem}_{k}(\beta,\gamma)\rvert\le\frac{M_{3,k}}{6}(\beta\norm{\hatF_{k}}+\gamma\norm{\hatG_{k}})^{3}$.
\end{lemma}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift_taylor} is the pathwise second-order Taylor expansion for each realized oracle pair.
Its linear and quadratic terms define the five scalar coefficients
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift_terms_def}
\begin{aligned}
e_{k}&\triangleq\inner{\nabla\Vk(\bz_{k})}{\hatF_{k}},
\quad
d_{k}\triangleq -\inner{\nabla\Vk(\bz_{k})}{\hatG_{k}},\\
c_{k}&\triangleq\hatF_{k}^{\top}\nabla^{2}\Vk(\bz_{k})\hatF_{k},
\quad
a_{k}\triangleq\hatG_{k}^{\top}\nabla^{2}\Vk(\bz_{k})\hatG_{k},\\
b_{k}&\triangleq\hatF_{k}^{\top}\nabla^{2}\Vk(\bz_{k})\hatG_{k}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift_terms_def} is pathwise: it uses the realized oracle pair $(\hatF_k,\hatG_k)$ rather than the population directions $(\bF(\bz_k),\bG(\bz_k))$.
Define its reference quadratic once as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qref}
q_k(\beta,\gamma)
\triangleq
-\beta e_k-\gamma d_k
+\tfrac12c_k\beta^2-b_k\beta\gamma
+\tfrac12a_k\gamma^2.
\end{equation}
Thus, Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift_taylor} is $\mathscr I_k(\beta,\gamma)=q_k(\beta,\gamma)+\mathrm{rem}_k(\beta,\gamma)$.
Here $e_k,d_k$ are first-order predicted decreases along $-\hatF_k,+\hatG_k$, $c_k,a_k$ are their directional curvatures, and $b_k$ is the cross-curvature.
Assumptions~\ref{ass:local_reg} and~\ref{ass:oracles} respectively control the third-order remainder and the conditional oracle errors used in the expected-drift analysis.

\subsection{Closed-Form Step-Size Selection}\label{subsec:closed_form}
Discarding the bounded third-order remainder in Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift_taylor} yields a bivariate quadratic model for selecting $(\beta,\gamma)$.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift_terms_def} defines reference coefficients from $\nabla\Vk(\bz_k)$ and directional Hessian actions.
We denote their evaluated counterparts returned by the local-model routine by $(\tilde e_k,\tilde d_k,\tilde c_k,\tilde a_k,\tilde b_k)$.
Exact automatic differentiation gives $(\tilde e_k,\tilde d_k,\tilde c_k,\tilde a_k,\tilde b_k)=(e_k,d_k,c_k,a_k,b_k)$, whereas finite-difference or directional-secant implementations, possibly combined with moving averages, generally give estimates such as $\tilde e_k\approx e_k$ and $\tilde c_k\approx c_k$.
The quadratic coefficient matrix assembled from the evaluated values is $\widetilde{\bH}_k\triangleq\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\tilde c_k&-\tilde b_k\\-\tilde b_k&\tilde a_k\end{smallmatrix}\right]$.
Because local nonconvexity or evaluation error may make $\widetilde{\bH}_k$ indefinite or nearly singular, let $\lambda_{\min}(\cdot)$ denote the smallest eigenvalue, fix a positive-definiteness floor $\lambda_{\rm pd}>0$, define $[u]_+\triangleq\max\{u,0\}$, and apply the common spectral shift
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral_shift}
\lambda_k=[\lambda_{\rm pd}-\lambda_{\min}(\widetilde{\bH}_k)]_+,
\quad
\widehat{\bH}_k=\widetilde{\bH}_k+\lambda_k\Id_2.
\end{equation}
Here $\lambda_k$ is the smallest nonnegative common diagonal correction that raises the minimum eigenvalue to at least $\lambda_{\rm pd}$.
We denote the resulting numerically stabilized coefficients by hats: $\hat e_k=\tilde e_k$, $\hat d_k=\tilde d_k$, $\hat b_k=\tilde b_k$, and $(\hat c_k,\hat a_k)=(\tilde c_k+\lambda_k,\tilde a_k+\lambda_k)$.
Equivalently, $\rho_{c,k}=\rho_{a,k}=\lambda_k\ge0$ add $\tfrac12\rho_{c,k}\beta^2+\tfrac12\rho_{a,k}\gamma^2$ to the tilde-coefficient model while leaving its linear and cross terms unchanged.
The implemented model is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qhat}
\begin{aligned}
\hat q_{k}(\beta,\gamma)
&\triangleq
-\beta\hat e_{k}-\gamma\hat d_{k}
+\tfrac{1}{2}\hat c_{k}\beta^{2}-\hat b_{k}\beta\gamma\\
&\quad+\tfrac{1}{2}\hat a_{k}\gamma^{2},
\quad (\beta,\gamma)\in[0,\infty)^{2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Its stabilized Hessian is $\widehat{\bH}_k$, and $\hat\delta_k\triangleq\det\widehat{\bH}_k=\hat c_k\hat a_k-\hat b_k^2$.

Eq.~\eqref{eq:spectral_shift} gives $\lambda_{\min}(\widehat{\bH}_k)=\max\{\lambda_{\min}(\widetilde{\bH}_k),\lambda_{\rm pd}\}\ge\lambda_{\rm pd}$.
Thus, $\widehat{\bH}_k\succ0$, and its leading principal minors satisfy $\hat c_k>0$ and $\hat\delta_k>0$.
The controller thus solves the strictly convex two-variable quadratic program $\min_{(\beta,\gamma)\in[0,\infty)^2}\hat q_k(\beta,\gamma)$.
Its $2\times2$ Hessian permits an explicit interior solution together with a finite set of boundary candidates.
The unconstrained critical point is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unconstrained_pair}
(\beta_{k}^{\rm uc},\gamma_{k}^{\rm uc})
\triangleq
\Bigl(\tfrac{\hat a_{k}\hat e_{k}+\hat b_{k}\hat d_{k}}{\hat\delta_{k}},\,
\tfrac{\hat b_{k}\hat e_{k}+\hat c_{k}\hat d_{k}}{\hat\delta_{k}}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
and the boundary candidates are $\bigl([\hat e_{k}/\hat c_{k}]_{+},0\bigr)$, $\bigl(0,[\hat d_{k}/\hat a_{k}]_{+}\bigr)$, $(0,0)$.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-form two-direction surrogate controller]\label{prop:closed_form}
The implemented problem in Eq.~\eqref{eq:qhat} admits a unique minimizer, given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:unconstrained_pair} when both components are nonnegative, and otherwise by the boundary candidate with smallest $\hat q_{k}$.
When the unconstrained critical point belongs to $[0,\infty)^2$, the implemented predicted decrease $\widehat\Delta_{k}^{\star}\triangleq -\hat q_{k}(\beta_{k}^{\star},\gamma_{k}^{\star})$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Delta_hat_interior}
\widehat\Delta_{k}^{\star}=\frac{\hat a_{k}\hat e_{k}^{2}+2\hat b_{k}\hat e_{k}\hat d_{k}+\hat c_{k}\hat d_{k}^{2}}{2\hat\delta_{k}}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

This gives the uncapped closed form; the practical step-size safeguards require the exact box solution below.

\begin{proposition}[Exact box-constrained fallback]\label{prop:box_fallback}
Fix finite caps $\beta_{\max},\gamma_{\max}>0$.
The unique minimizer of $\hat q_k$ on $[0,\beta_{\max}]\times[0,\gamma_{\max}]$ is the member with smallest objective value among the feasible unconstrained point in Eq.~\eqref{eq:unconstrained_pair} and the four edge minimizers
\begin{align*}
&\left(\left[\hat e_k/\hat c_k\right]_{[0,\beta_{\max}]},0\right),\quad
\left(0,\left[\hat d_k/\hat a_k\right]_{[0,\gamma_{\max}]}\right),\\
&\left(\beta_{\max},
\left[(\hat d_k+\hat b_k\beta_{\max})/\hat a_k\right]_{[0,\gamma_{\max}]}\right),\\
&\left(\left[(\hat e_k+\hat b_k\gamma_{\max})/\hat c_k\right]_{[0,\beta_{\max}]},
\gamma_{\max}\right),
\end{align*}
where $[u]_{[l,r]}\triangleq\min\{r,\max\{l,u\}\}$.
Consequently, its predicted decrease is at least as large as that of every one-direction or fixed-coupling comparator whose step-size pair lies in the same box.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Strict convexity gives a unique minimizer.
If it is in the interior, the gradient equations give Eq.~\eqref{eq:unconstrained_pair}; otherwise it lies on one of the four edges, and differentiating the resulting one-dimensional strictly convex quadratic and clipping its stationary point to that edge gives the four displayed candidates.
Set inclusion then proves the stated comparison.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Model-Level Curvature Improvement}\label{subsec:dominance}

The closed-form rule makes the two-direction update computationally explicit.
Its model-level benefit is determined by comparing the optimized quadratic decrease with the decreases attainable after restricting the feasible step-size pair.

\begin{proposition}[Predicted-decrease comparison]\label{prop:dominance}
Fix $s_{\max}>0$ and define
\begin{align*}
\widehat\Delta_{k}^{\bF}
&\triangleq
-\min_{\beta\ge 0}\hat q_{k}(\beta,0)
=
\tfrac{[\hat e_{k}]_{+}^{2}}{2\hat c_{k}},\\
\widehat\Delta_{k}^{\bG}
&\triangleq
-\min_{\gamma\ge 0}\hat q_{k}(0,\gamma)
=
\tfrac{[\hat d_{k}]_{+}^{2}}{2\hat a_{k}},\\
\widehat\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{2nd}}
&\triangleq
-\min_{s\in[0,s_{\max}]}\hat q_{k}(s,s^{2}),
\end{align*}
Here $\widehat\Delta_k^{\mathrm{2nd}}$ is the PPM/EGM-style second-order coupling comparator $(\beta,\gamma)=(s,s^2)$.
Then $\widehat\Delta_{k}^{\star}\ge\max\{\widehat\Delta_{k}^{\bF},\widehat\Delta_{k}^{\bG},\widehat\Delta_{k}^{\mathrm{2nd}}\}$ almost surely.
Moreover, when the unconstrained critical point in Eq.~\eqref{eq:unconstrained_pair} belongs to $[0,\infty)^2$, the gap over the pure-field rule is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dominance_gap}
\widehat\Delta_{k}^{\star}-\widehat\Delta_{k}^{\bF}
=
\frac{(\hat b_{k}\hat e_{k}+\hat c_{k}\hat d_{k})^{2}}{2\hat c_{k}\hat\delta_{k}}
+\frac{[-\hat e_k]_{+}^{2}}{2\hat c_k}
\ge 0,
\end{equation}
which is strictly positive if and only if either $\hat b_{k}\hat e_{k}+\hat c_{k}\hat d_{k}\neq 0$ or $\hat e_k<0$.
In the usual field-descent regime $\hat e_k\ge0$, strict improvement is therefore equivalent to the curvature direction having a nonzero reduced gradient after projecting out the field direction in the $\widehat{\bH}_{k}$-metric.
\end{proposition}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:dominance_gap} measures the curvature step's extra predicted decrease through the reduced gradient $\hat b_k\hat e_k+\hat c_k\hat d_k$ left after accounting for the field direction.
For collinear directions, the common spectral shift makes the Hessian nonsingular, but any gain then comes from step-size constraints or regularization because the direction span is unchanged.
Section~\ref{sec:conv} combines this comparator dominance with the field-geometry certificate and explicitly propagates stochastic-oracle, model-estimation, Taylor-remainder, and safeguard errors into finite-time guarantees.

% ============================================================
\section{Finite-Time Stationarity Analysis}\label{sec:conv}
% ============================================================
Section~\ref{sec:drift} constructs the controller and characterizes its predicted quadratic decrease.
We now bound its realized and conditional Lyapunov changes, combine geometric coverage with box-QP dominance for finite-time stationarity, and then transfer stationarity to policy-space performance.

\subsection{From Field Geometry to Conditional Drift}\label{subsec:pathwise}

\begin{assumption}[Local admissible region]\label{ass:local}
There exist an open set $\cN$ whose compact closure satisfies $\overline{\cN}\subset\cD$ and an index $k_{0}\ge0$ such that $\cN$ contains the local stationary component of interest, $\cP$ is $C^3$ on a neighborhood of $\overline{\cN}$, and $\bz_k\in\cN$ for every $k\ge k_0$ almost surely.
\end{assumption}

This is a local-invariance condition: the safeguards in Section~\ref{sec:impl} keep the accepted trajectory in a prescribed implementable region, and the analysis applies when that trajectory remains in $\cN$.
On this region, define the geometric coverage modulus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coverage_modulus}
m_{\rm geo}
\triangleq
\inf_{\substack{\bz\in\cN\\\Phi(\bz)>0}}
\frac{\max\{\chi_F(\bz),\chi_G(\bz)\}}{\Phi(\bz)}.
\end{equation}
The stationarity result below applies when $m_{\rm geo}>0$, meaning that at every nonstationary point at least one available coordinate supplies a uniformly proportional field-energy decrease.
Unlike a gradient-aligned field, a general saddle field does not make the positivity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:coverage_modulus} automatic; the modulus is the quantitative step that converts directional field-energy decrease into the stationarity residual $\Phi$.

The following condition derives neighborhood coverage from the linearization at the target stationary point; $\sigma_{\min}(\cdot)$ denotes the smallest singular value.
\begin{proposition}[A sufficient condition for geometric coverage]\label{prop:coverage_sufficient}
Fix a target stationary point $\bz^\star\in\mathcal Z^\star$ and define its metric-transformed Jacobian $\widetilde{\bA}_\star\triangleq\bW_V^{1/2}\bA(\bz^\star)\bW_V^{-1/2}$.
If $\sym(\widetilde{\bA}_\star)\succeq0$ and $\sigma_{\min}(\widetilde{\bA}_\star)>0$, then there exist a constant $c_\star>0$ and an open neighborhood $\cN_\star$ of $\bz^\star$, with $\overline{\cN_\star}\subset\cD$, such that
\(\max\{\chi_F(\bz),\chi_G(\bz)\}\ge c_\star\Phi(\bz)\) for every $\bz\in\cN_\star$.
Consequently, if the admissible region in Assumption~\ref{ass:local} is chosen with $\overline{\cN}\subset\cN_\star$, then $m_{\rm geo}\ge c_\star>0$.
\end{proposition}

For $\bW_V=\Id$, max-variable concavity and min-variable convexity at $\bz^\star$ give the positive-semidefinite condition, while nonsingularity gives a nondegenerate target linearization; continuity then supplies neighborhood coverage, including pure rotation with $\sym(\widetilde{\bA}_\star)=0$.
For the normal block $\bA=\mu\Id_2+\sigma J_0$, the exact ratio in Eq.~\eqref{eq:coverage_modulus} is $\max\{\mu,\sigma^2-\mu^2\}$; in particular, $\mu=0$ gives $m_{\rm geo}=\sigma^2$.

Compactness and smoothness give the finite performance-direction bound
\[
B_{P,D}
\triangleq
\sup_{\bz\in\overline{\cN}}
\max\!\left\{
\left|\inner{\nabla\cP}{\bF}\right|,
\left|\inner{\nabla\cP}{\bG}\right|
\right\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Geometry-to-drift certificate]\label{lem:geometry_certificate}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:local}, suppose the modulus in Eq.~\eqref{eq:coverage_modulus} satisfies $m_{\rm geo}>0$.
Define the geometric descent coefficient $\mu_{\rm geo}$ and policy-component perturbation $r_P$ by
\[
\mu_{\rm geo}\triangleq\frac{\lambda_Fm_{\rm geo}}{\kappa_F},
\quad
r_P\triangleq\frac{\lambda_PB_{P,D}}{\kappa_P}.
\]
Then, for every $\bz\in\cN$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derived_drift_certificate}
\max\{D_F(\bz),D_G(\bz)\}
\ge
\mu_{\rm geo}\Phi(\bz)-r_P.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

Proposition~\ref{prop:coverage_sufficient} and Lemma~\ref{lem:geometry_certificate} form the first bridge: target-Jacobian conditions produce neighborhood coverage, which converts one of the two field-energy signals into the composite decrease in Eq.~\eqref{eq:derived_drift_certificate} without directly assuming uniform alignment with $-\nabla\Vk$.
The policy component contributes only the finite perturbation $r_P$, which vanishes for $\lambda_P=0$ and otherwise enters the residual floor.

Because $\overline{\cN}$ is compactly contained in $\cD$, continuity yields uniform constants $G_V,H_V,B_F,B_G\ge0$ such that $\norm{\nabla\Vk}\le G_V$, $\norm{\nabla^2\Vk}\le H_V$, $\norm{\bF}\le B_F$, and $\norm{\bG}\le B_G$ on $\cN$.
In particular, $G_{V,k},H_{V,k}$ may be replaced by $G_V,H_V$ for $k\ge k_0$.

Recall the pathwise reference quadratic $q_k$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:qref} and the stabilized implemented quadratic $\hat q_k$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:qhat}.
For fixed caps $\beta_{\max},\gamma_{\max}>0$ and an implemented pair $(\beta_k,\gamma_k)\in[0,\beta_{\max}]\times[0,\gamma_{\max}]$, define the box-optimal predicted decrease
\[
\widehat\Delta_k^{\rm box}
\triangleq
-\min_{0\le\beta\le\beta_{\max},\,0\le\gamma\le\gamma_{\max}}
\hat q_k(\beta,\gamma).
\]
The implemented model $\hat q_k$ uses the stabilized coefficients, and one-sided residuals measure model discrepancy and accepted-step suboptimality.
Define the cubic Taylor remainder $\varepsilon_{k}^{\mathrm{rem}}\triangleq M_{3,k} (\beta_{k}\norm{\hatF_{k}}+\gamma_{k}\norm{\hatG_{k}})^{3}/6$.
Let $\varepsilon_k^{\rm model}\ge0$ be $\mathscr F_{k+1}$-measurable and satisfy $q_k(\beta_k,\gamma_k)\le\hat q_k(\beta_k,\gamma_k) +\varepsilon_k^{\rm model}$.
For the implemented box problem, define $\varepsilon_k^{\rm step,box}\triangleq\hat q_k(\beta_k,\gamma_k)+\widehat\Delta_k^{\rm box}\ge0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps_tot}
\varepsilon_{k}^{\mathrm{box}}
\triangleq
\varepsilon_{k}^{\mathrm{rem}}+\varepsilon_{k}^{\mathrm{model}}
+\varepsilon_{k}^{\mathrm{step,box}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[One-sided local-model perturbation bounds]\label{prop:model_approx}
Suppose the raw evaluated coefficients satisfy $\lvert\tilde e_{k}-e_{k}\rvert\le\delta_{e,k}$, $\lvert\tilde d_{k}-d_{k}\rvert\le\delta_{d,k}$, $\lvert\tilde c_{k}-c_{k}\rvert\le\delta_{c,k}$, $\lvert\tilde a_{k}-a_{k}\rvert\le\delta_{a,k}$, and $\lvert\tilde b_{k}-b_{k}\rvert\le\delta_{b,k}$ for nonnegative error bounds.
Then, for every feasible $(\beta,\gamma)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps_model_bound}
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_k^{\rm model}(\beta,\gamma)
&\triangleq\beta\delta_{e,k}+\gamma\delta_{d,k}
+\tfrac12\beta^2\delta_{c,k}+\beta\gamma\delta_{b,k}
+\tfrac12\gamma^2\delta_{a,k},\\
\varepsilon_k^{\rm cmp}(\beta,\gamma)
&\triangleq\varepsilon_k^{\rm model}(\beta,\gamma)
+\tfrac12\rho_{c,k}\beta^2+\tfrac12\rho_{a,k}\gamma^2,\\
q_k-\hat q_k&\le\varepsilon_k^{\rm model},
\quad \hat q_k-q_k\le\varepsilon_k^{\rm cmp}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In the last line, all arguments $(\beta,\gamma)$ are suppressed, and the superscript $\mathrm{cmp}$ denotes the comparator-side bound.
In particular, the one-sided relation used in Eq.~\eqref{eq:eps_tot} holds with $\varepsilon_k^{\mathrm{model}}=\varepsilon_k^{\mathrm{model}}(\beta_k,\gamma_k)$.
\end{proposition}

Exact automatic differentiation makes the raw coefficient errors and the selected-step residual $\varepsilon_k^{\rm model}$ zero because the spectral shift only raises $\hat q_k$; the comparator-side bound retains its explicit shift cost, and an exact box-QP solution makes the box-step residual zero.

\begin{lemma}[Pathwise one-step Lyapunov bound]\label{lem:pathwise_drift}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:local_reg}, for every $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathwise_drift}
\Vk(\bz_{k+1})-\Vk(\bz_{k})
\le -\widehat\Delta_{k}^{\rm box}+\varepsilon_{k}^{\rm box}
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

This inequality separates the decrease predicted by the QP from stochastic, modeling, and safeguard residuals.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional Taylor-remainder bound]\label{lem:residual_budget}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:oracles} and~\ref{ass:local_reg} hold and $\beta_{k}\le\beta_{\max}$, $\gamma_{k}\le\gamma_{\max}$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps_rem_cond}
\E[\varepsilon_{k}^{\mathrm{rem}}\mid\mathscr{F}_{k}]
\le
\frac{2M_{3,k}}{3}(\beta_{\max}+\gamma_{\max})^{3}\sigma_{3,k}^{3}.
\end{equation}
If $M_{3,k}\le\bar M_{3}$ and $\sigma_{3,k}\le\bar\sigma_{3}$, then $\E[\varepsilon_{k}^{\mathrm{rem}}\mid\mathscr F_k]\le\bar R_{\rm rem}$ almost surely, where $\bar R_{\rm rem}\triangleq(2\bar M_3/3)(\beta_{\max}+\gamma_{\max})^3\bar\sigma_3^3$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{assumption}[Uniform local budgets]\label{ass:budget}
There exist finite nonnegative constants appearing below such that, for every $k\ge k_0$, the following hold:
(a) $\bar b_k^F\le\bar b_F$, $\bar b_k^G\le\bar b_G$, $\sigma_{2,k}\le\bar\sigma_2$, and $\sigma_{3,k}\le\bar\sigma_3$;
(b) the common spectral shift satisfies $\rho_{c,k}=\rho_{a,k}\le\bar\rho$, and the raw coefficient-evaluation errors in Proposition~\ref{prop:model_approx} satisfy
\[
\begin{gathered}
\E[\delta_{e,k}\mid\mathscr F_k]\le\bar\delta_e,
\quad
\E[\delta_{d,k}\mid\mathscr F_k]\le\bar\delta_d,\\
\E[\delta_{c,k}\mid\mathscr F_k]\le\bar\delta_c,
\quad
\E[\delta_{a,k}\mid\mathscr F_k]\le\bar\delta_a,
\quad
\E[\delta_{b,k}\mid\mathscr F_k]\le\bar\delta_b;
\end{gathered}
\]
(c) $M_{3,k}\le\bar M_3$ and $\E[\varepsilon_k^{\rm step,box}\mid\mathscr F_k]\le\bar R_{\rm step}$ almost surely.
\end{assumption}

Define the derived oracle-moment bounds and aggregate residual budgets
\[
\begin{gathered}
M_F\triangleq3B_F^2+3\bar b_F^2+3\bar\sigma_2^2,
\quad
M_G\triangleq3B_G^2+3\bar b_G^2+3\bar\sigma_2^2,\\
\bar b_D\triangleq\max\{\bar b_F,\bar b_G\},
\quad
M_D\triangleq\max\{M_F,M_G\},\\
\bar\delta_1\triangleq\max\{\bar\delta_e,\bar\delta_d\},
\quad
\bar\delta_2\triangleq\max\{\bar\delta_c,\bar\delta_a\},\\
\bar R_{\rm model}\triangleq{}
\beta_{\max}\bar\delta_e+\gamma_{\max}\bar\delta_d
+\tfrac12\beta_{\max}^2\bar\delta_c\\
\quad{}+\beta_{\max}\gamma_{\max}\bar\delta_b
+\tfrac12\gamma_{\max}^2\bar\delta_a,\\
\bar R_{\rm box}\triangleq\bar R_{\rm rem}+\bar R_{\rm model}+\bar R_{\rm step}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual_budget}, Proposition~\ref{prop:model_approx}, the caps, and Assumption~\ref{ass:budget} give $\E[\varepsilon_k^{\rm box}\mid\mathscr F_k]\le\bar R_{\rm box}$ almost surely, so the aggregate is derived from its three displayed sources.
For a comparator scale $s\in[0,\min\{\beta_{\max},\gamma_{\max}\}]$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R_direction}
\mathcal R_D(s)
\triangleq
s(G_V\bar b_D+\bar\delta_1)
+\tfrac12s^2(H_VM_D+\bar\rho+\bar\delta_2)
+\bar R_{\rm box}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional Lyapunov-drift comparator bound]\label{lem:cond_drift}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:smooth}, \ref{ass:oracles}, \ref{ass:local_reg}, \ref{ass:local}, and~\ref{ass:budget} hold.
Let $\jmath_k\in\{F,G\}$ be any $\mathscr F_k$-measurable selector and write $D_{\jmath_k}=D_F$ or $D_G$ accordingly.
Then, for every admissible deterministic comparator scale $s$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cond_drift}
\mathscr D_k
\le
-sD_{\jmath_k}(\bz_k)
+\mathcal R_D(s).
\end{equation}
Thus, the implemented update inherits, up to the explicit residual budget, the conditional drift decrease certified by either pure coordinate.
\end{lemma}

The selector is used only in the proof; the implemented box QP optimizes over both pure-coordinate comparators and their combinations.
This second bridge uses QP optimality and the four explicit residual budgets to lift a comparison on each realized stochastic stencil to the conditional-drift bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cond_drift}.

\subsection{Finite-Time Stationarity Guarantee}\label{subsec:ergodic}

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem: Geometry-Adaptive Finite-Time Stationarity]\label{thm:ergodic}
Suppose Assumptions~\ref{ass:smooth}, \ref{ass:oracles}, \ref{ass:local_reg}, \ref{ass:local}, and~\ref{ass:budget} hold, and the modulus in Eq.~\eqref{eq:coverage_modulus} satisfies $m_{\rm geo}>0$.
For any comparator scale $s\in(0,\min\{\beta_{\max},\gamma_{\max}\}]$, define $\mathcal R_{\rm geo}(s)\triangleq\mathcal R_D(s)+sr_P$.
Then, for every integer $K>k_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main_stationarity}
\frac{1}{K-k_{0}}
\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{K-1}\E[\Phi(\bz_{k})]
\le
\frac{\E[\Vk(\bz_{k_{0}})]}{s\mu_{\rm geo}(K-k_{0})}
+
\frac{\mathcal R_{\rm geo}(s)}{s\mu_{\rm geo}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

For fixed admissible $s$ and $K$-independent budgets, Eq.~\eqref{eq:main_stationarity} gives an $O(1/K)$ transient to an explicit floor containing the oracle, model, Taylor-remainder, policy-component, and safeguard errors.
The signals $\chi_F$ and $\chi_G$ respectively certify contractive symmetric fields and pure rotation, and box-QP dominance inherits either certificate.
For an independent uniform index $I_K\in\{k_0,\ldots,K-1\}$, the left side is $\E[\Phi(\bz_{I_K})]$, so no parameter averaging is required.
Increasing the analysis comparator $s$ strengthens nominal decrease but enlarges higher-order terms, whereas decreasing it magnifies any $s$-independent residual after normalization; the implemented controller itself still selects $(\beta_k,\gamma_k)$ from the box QP.
The result is therefore local and ergodic, not a claim that curvature always descends or that convergence is global.

\subsection{Policy-Space Performance Guarantee}\label{subsec:performance_guarantee}

Exploitability and robust return are policy-space quantities beyond parameter-space stationarity.
We therefore transfer Lyapunov control to these performance measures.
For stationary randomized policies, define the maximizing player's worst-case return, game value, and unregularized Nash gap (reported below as exploitability), where the subscript ${\rm BR}$ denotes best response:
\begin{align}
R_{\rm BR}(\pi)&\triangleq\inf_{\bar\nu}J(\pi,\bar\nu),\notag\\
v^{\star}&\triangleq\sup_{\bar\pi}R_{\rm BR}(\bar\pi),\notag\\
\operatorname{Gap}_{0}(\pi,\nu)
&\triangleq
\sup_{\bar\pi}J(\bar\pi,\nu)-\inf_{\bar\nu}J(\pi,\bar\nu).
\label{eq:global_gap}
\end{align}
The following proposition links the entropy-regularized gap used by the controller to the original zero-sum Markov-game objective.

\begin{proposition}[Policy-space performance bridge]\label{prop:performance_bridge}
Suppose $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ are finite.
For every policy pair $(\pi,\nu)$ and $\varepsilon\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:performance_bridge}
\begin{aligned}
0&\le v^{\star}-R_{\rm BR}(\pi)
\le \operatorname{Gap}_{0}(\pi,\nu)\\
&\le \operatorname{Gap}_{\varepsilon}(\pi,\nu)
+\frac{\varepsilon\bigl(\log|\mathcal A|+\log|\mathcal B|\bigr)}{1-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The definitions in Eq.~\eqref{eq:global_gap} give the first inequality, while $v^{\star}\le\sup_{\bar\pi}J(\bar\pi,\nu)$ gives the second.
Write $A_\alpha=\log|\mathcal A|/(1-\alpha)$ and $B_\alpha=\log|\mathcal B|/(1-\alpha)$.
The per-state entropy bound gives $0\le\mathsf H_\alpha^\pi\le A_\alpha$ and $0\le\mathsf H_\alpha^\nu\le B_\alpha$ for every policy pair.
These bounds imply
\begin{align*}
\sup_{\bar\pi}J(\bar\pi,\nu)
&\le \sup_{\bar\pi}J_\varepsilon(\bar\pi,\nu)+\varepsilon B_\alpha,\\
-\inf_{\bar\nu}J(\pi,\bar\nu)
&\le -\inf_{\bar\nu}J_\varepsilon(\pi,\bar\nu)+\varepsilon A_\alpha.
\end{align*}
Adding the last two inequalities gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:performance_bridge}.
\end{proof}

Thus the regularized gap controls the maximizing player's unregularized worst-case-return loss up to the stated entropy term.

\begin{proposition}[Smooth finite-game performance component]\label{prop:smooth_entropy_gap}
Suppose $\mathcal S,\mathcal A,\mathcal B$ are finite, the one-stage rewards are bounded, $\varepsilon>0$, and, on the parameter neighborhood under consideration, the current policies are $C^3$ with action probabilities bounded away from zero.
Then the entropy-regularized best-response values against either current player, and hence $\operatorname{Gap}_\varepsilon$, are $C^3$ functions of the current policy parameters.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix the minimizing-player policy.
The maximizing player's soft Bellman value satisfies $v=T_\varepsilon(v;\bpsi)$, where each component of $T_\varepsilon$ is an $\varepsilon\log\sum\exp$ of the action values induced by $\nu_{\bpsi}$.
Explicitly,
\begin{align*}
[T_\varepsilon(v;\bpsi)]_s
&=\varepsilon\log\sum_a \exp\!\left(\frac{Q_\psi^v(s,a)}
{\varepsilon}\right),\\
Q_\psi^v(s,a)
&\triangleq \bar r_\psi(s,a)
+\alpha\sum_{s'}\bar P_\psi(s'|s,a)v_{s'} .
\end{align*}
Here $\mathcal H(p)\triangleq-\sum_i p_i\log p_i$ is the per-state Shannon entropy, $\bar P_\psi$ averages $P$ over $\nu_\psi$, and $\bar r_\psi$ averages $r$ while including the opponent-entropy term $-\varepsilon\mathcal H(\nu_\psi(\cdot|s))$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:entropic_return}.
The map is $C^3$ in $(v,\bpsi)$, and its Jacobian with respect to $v$ is $\alpha P_{\pi_{\rm BR},\nu_{\bpsi}}$, where $\pi_{\rm BR}$ is the maximizing player's regularized best response and $P_{\pi_{\rm BR},\nu_{\bpsi}}$ is the induced transition matrix.
Thus, with $D_v$ denoting differentiation in $v$, $\|D_vT_\varepsilon\|_\infty\le\alpha<1$.
Hence $\Id-D_vT_\varepsilon$ is invertible by the Neumann series, and the implicit-function theorem makes the unique soft fixed point $C^3$ in $\bpsi$.
The minimizing player's soft-min Bellman equation follows after replacing $\log\sum\exp$ by $-\log\sum\exp(-\cdot)$.
Multiplication by $\rho$ gives the two regularized best-response values, whose difference is $\operatorname{Gap}_\varepsilon$.
\end{proof}

Consequently, choosing $\cP=\operatorname{Gap}_\varepsilon$ gives a concrete smooth performance component for Definition~\ref{def:Lyap} in finite Markov games.

\begin{assumption}[Local policy-space error bound]\label{ass:performance_error_bound}
There exist constants $c_{\rm gap}>0$ and $\xi_{\rm gap}\ge0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap_error_bound}
\operatorname{Gap}_\varepsilon(\bz)
\le
c_{\rm gap}\Phi(\bz)+\xi_{\rm gap},
\quad
\bz\in\cN.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:gap_error_bound} is a local Polyak--\L{}ojasiewicz-type policy error bound \cite{Karimi2016PL}.
The slack $\xi_{\rm gap}$ permits parameter-stationary points with nonzero policy-space exploitability, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:gap_error_bound} does not require the two zero sets to coincide.

\begin{corollary}[Ergodic policy-space performance]\label{cor:ergodic_performance}
Suppose the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:ergodic} and Assumption~\ref{ass:performance_error_bound} hold, and $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ are finite.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ergodic_performance}
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{1}{K-k_0}\sum_{k=k_0}^{K-1}
\E[v^\star-R_{\rm BR}(\pi_{\btheta_k})]\\
&\le
\frac{1}{K-k_0}\sum_{k=k_0}^{K-1}
\E[\operatorname{Gap}_0(\pi_{\btheta_k},\nu_{\bpsi_k})]\\
&\le
c_{\rm gap}\!\left[
\frac{\E[\Vk(\bz_{k_0})]}{s\mu_{\rm geo}(K-k_0)}
+\frac{\mathcal R_{\rm geo}(s)}{s\mu_{\rm geo}}
\right]
+\xi_{\rm gap}+B_\varepsilon,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $B_\varepsilon\triangleq\varepsilon(\log|\mathcal A|+\log|\mathcal B|)/(1-\alpha)$ is the entropy-regularization bias bound.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Average Eq.~\eqref{eq:gap_error_bound}, apply Theorem~\ref{thm:ergodic}, and then use Proposition~\ref{prop:performance_bridge} pointwise.
\end{proof}

Thus, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ergodic_performance} gives the policy-space consequence of the main stationarity theorem under the local error bound with slack.

\section{Practical Implementation}\label{sec:impl}
% ============================================================
This section specifies how the local quantities required by the controller can be evaluated in practice.
We consider exact automatic differentiation, directional-secant approximations, and safeguarded box-constrained updates.

\subsection{Exact local-model evaluation}
The reference pathwise terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift_terms_def} are assembled from one field oracle $\hatF_{k}$, one curvature oracle $\hatG_{k}$, one gradient $\nabla\Vk(\bz_k)$, and the Hessian--vector products of $\Vk$ along $\hatF_k$ and $\hatG_k$.
Automatic differentiation can pass through a fixed differentiable inner loop for $\cP$, while implicit differentiation handles an exact local envelope.
Because $\cE_F$ contains $\bF^\top\bW_V\bF/2$, exact Hessian--vector products of $\Vk$ generally involve third derivatives of the game objective; the secant realization below avoids forming them.
Exact automatic differentiation gives $(\tilde e_k,\tilde d_k,\tilde c_k,\tilde a_k,\tilde b_k)=(e_k,d_k,c_k,a_k,b_k)$.
After exact coefficient evaluation, $\hat q_k-q_k=\lambda_k(\beta^2+\gamma^2)/2\ge0$; hence the selected-step one-sided model residual is zero, while the comparator-side bound retains the explicit shift.

\subsection{Approximate local-model estimation}
For probe radii $\delta_{F,k},\delta_{G,k}>0$, a directional-secant realization holds $(\hatF_k,\hatG_k)$ fixed and evaluates $V_{\pm F}=\Vk(\bz_k\pm\delta_{F,k}\hatF_k)$, $V_{\pm G}=\Vk(\bz_k\pm\delta_{G,k}\hatG_k)$, and $V_{\pm F,\pm G}=\Vk(\bz_k\pm\delta_{F,k}\hatF_k \pm\delta_{G,k}\hatG_k)$.
Writing $V_0=\Vk(\bz_k)$, the central stencils give the tilde approximations
\[
\begin{gathered}
\tilde e_k=\frac{V_{+F}-V_{-F}}{2\delta_{F,k}},\quad
\tilde d_k=-\frac{V_{+G}-V_{-G}}{2\delta_{G,k}},\\
\tilde c_k=\frac{V_{+F}-2V_0+V_{-F}}{\delta_{F,k}^2},\quad
\tilde a_k=\frac{V_{+G}-2V_0+V_{-G}}{\delta_{G,k}^2},\\
\tilde b_k=\frac{V_{+F,+G}-V_{+F,-G}-V_{-F,+G}+V_{-F,-G}}
{4\delta_{F,k}\delta_{G,k}},
\end{gathered}
\]
before the spectral shift in Section~\ref{subsec:closed_form} is applied.
Writing $\hatF(\bz,\zeta)$ for the field oracle evaluated at $\bz$ with random seed $\zeta$, a shared seed $\zeta_k$ and radius $\epsilon_k>0$ give the Jacobian--vector finite-difference curvature oracle $\hatG_{k}^{\mathrm{fd}}=[\hatF(\bz_{k}+\epsilon_{k}\hatF_{k},\zeta_{k})-\hatF(\bz_{k},\zeta_{k})]/\epsilon_{k}$.
A moment-based realization smooths the second-order terms by exponential moving averages while preserving the subproblem.

The effect of these approximation errors on the one-step model is quantified by Proposition~\ref{prop:model_approx}.

\subsection{Safeguarded computation}\label{subsec:safeguard}
With positive caps $\beta_{\max}$ and $\gamma_{\max}$, the implementation solves $\min_{(\beta,\gamma)\in[0,\beta_{\max}]\times[0,\gamma_{\max}]}\hat q_{k}(\beta,\gamma)$.
Any loss from approximate solution or backtracking relative to the box optimum is recorded in $\varepsilon_k^{\rm step,box}$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:ergodic}.
Each iteration queries $(\hatF_k,\hatG_k)$, evaluates the five tilde coefficients, applies the spectral shift, solves the two-dimensional box QP by Proposition~\ref{prop:box_fallback}, and applies Eq.~\eqref{eq:update}.
If the trial segment leaves a prescribed implementable trust region $\mathcal T\subset\cD$, a common backtracking factor shrinks $(\beta_k,\gamma_k)$; the analysis assumes the accepted trajectory lies in $\cN$, and records lost model decrease in $\varepsilon_k^{\mathrm{step,box}}$.
For bounded $\mathcal T$, the implementation reduces the probe radii as needed until every directional-stencil point lies in $\mathcal T$.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
% ============================================================

The experiments follow the same hierarchy as the analysis.
The normal-form study first isolates the symmetric--antisymmetric mechanism, the tabular and neural population-oracle studies then test policy-space behavior without trajectory noise, and the finite-trajectory study finally replaces exact expected directions by sampled stochastic oracles.
This progression separates geometric adaptation, nonlinear policy parameterization, and sampling error rather than confounding all three effects in a single benchmark.
Methods are the proposed box-QP controller with curvature (QP+G), its field-only ablation noG ($\gamma_k=0$), GDA, Adam-GDA \cite{KingmaBa2015Adam}, EGM, and three-inner-step PPM-3.
All methods update both players simultaneously; unless noted, fixed steps and QP caps are $0.03$, $\eta_\psi=1$ gives $\cE_F=\phi$, and population normalizers are frozen at initialization with floor $10^{-10}$.
For every Markov-game experiment, $J_{\rm alg}=J_\varepsilon$ with $(\alpha,\varepsilon)=(0.9,0.03)$; the regularized and unregularized value tolerances are $10^{-10}$ and $10^{-12}$, and the spectral floor is $10^{-8}$.
Across matched seeds, all paired $95\%$ confidence intervals are two-sided Student-$t$ intervals for metric-oriented differences, with positive values favoring QP+G.

\subsection{Controlled Normal-Form Geometry}\label{subsec:controlled_geometry}
We test the mechanism on the exactly solvable normal linear field with $\lambda_P=0$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:Vdef} retains only field energy.
For radial contraction rate $\mu\ge0$ and angular rotation rate $\sigma\ge0$,
\begin{align*}
\bF(\bz)&=\bA\bz,
&\bA&=\mu\Id_2+\sigma J_0,
&\Vk(\bz)&=\tfrac12\norm{\bF(\bz)}^2.
\end{align*}
Here $\bS=\mu\Id_2$, $\bW=\sigma J_0$, and direct calculation gives
\begin{align}
e&=\inner{\nabla\Vk}{\bF}
=\mu(\mu^2+\sigma^2)\norm{\bz}^2,\notag\\
d&=-\inner{\nabla\Vk}{\bG}
=(\mu^2+\sigma^2)(\sigma^2-\mu^2)\norm{\bz}^2.\label{eq:normal_d}
\end{align}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal_d} shows that $+\bG$ decreases the field energy exactly when $|\sigma|>|\mu|$, matching Theorem~\ref{thm:skew_dominance}; moreover, $\max\{\chi_F,\chi_G\}/\Phi=\max\{\mu,\sigma^2-\mu^2\}$ evaluates the coverage modulus in Eq.~\eqref{eq:coverage_modulus}.
For this quadratic field-energy function, the two-dimensional Taylor model is exact.

To quantify accepted curvature use, define its relative contribution as $C_k^G\triangleq\gamma_k\norm{\bG(\bz_k)}/\max\{\beta_k\norm{\bF(\bz_k)}+\gamma_k\norm{\bG(\bz_k)},10^{-15}\}$.
Fig.~\ref{fig:controlled_geometry} sweeps $\sigma/\mu\in[0,2.5]$; panels (a)--(c) recover the threshold and pure-rotation decay, while panel (d) shows first-update $\gamma_0/\gamma_{\max}$ and $C_0^G$ from the common initial point.
These quantities measure curvature-step activation and the curvature share of the additive nominal update magnitude; their threshold-like profile follows here from exact directions and $\lambda_P=0$.

\subsection{Tabular Markov Games with Population Oracles}\label{subsec:tabular}
We span a one-state normal-form benchmark and sequential finite-state games while removing function approximation, using rock--paper--scissors (RPS), five-state CyclicControl, and eight-state FrequencyHopping, each with three actions per player and tabular softmax logits.
RPS is a standard multi-agent RL benchmark \cite{Terry2021PettingZoo}; Appendix~\ref{app:environments} specifies the games.
For both the tabular and neural population experiments, the QP+G/noG controller uses the Markov-game composite Lyapunov function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vic_merit}
V_{\rm MG}(\bz)
=0.3\frac{\tfrac12\norm{\bF(\bz)}^{2}}{\kappa_F}
+\frac{\operatorname{Gap}_{\varepsilon}(\bz)}{\kappa_P},
\end{equation}
This corresponds to $\lambda_F=0.3$ and $\lambda_P=1$, with both normalizers frozen at initialization.
Population automatic differentiation computes $\bF$ as the saddle gradient of $J_\varepsilon$ and $\bG=\nabla\bF\,\bF$, each stencil recomputes both regularized BRs, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:performance_bridge} holds at every checkpoint.

Across ten initializations and $100$ joint updates, QP+G raises worst-case return and lowers exploitability relative to noG in all three games.
Exact one-sided paired sign tests of the return gains remain significant after Holm correction across the three games \cite{Holm1979}.
Fig.~\ref{fig:tabular} shows that the same ordering extends to the regularized gap and field norm, linking the performance gain to improved saddle-field optimization.

\subsection{Neural-Policy Markov Games with Population Oracles}\label{subsec:neural_mg}
The neural-policy experiment isolates nonlinear parameterization from sampling noise using separate $4$--$8$--$3$ tanh--softmax policies, forming a $134$-dimensional population field on the four games in Appendix~\ref{app:environments}.
An exact differentiable value-system solve supplies $\bF$ and $\bG=\nabla\bF\,\bF$; QP+G and noG evaluate Eq.~\eqref{eq:vic_merit} on nine- and three-point central stencils, respectively, with $\delta_D=\min\{10^{-2},10^{-3}/\max(\|D\|,10^{-12})\}$, $D\in\{\bF,\bG\}$.
Five screening seeds select each baseline rate from $\{0.001,0.003,0.01,0.03\}$; ten disjoint test seeds use $60$ simultaneous updates.
QP+G attains higher worst-case return and lower exploitability than noG in all four games, with positive lower endpoints in all paired $95\%$ confidence intervals for return gain; Fig.~\ref{fig:neural_mg} displays three games, and SecurityPatrol has the same mean ordering.
CyclicControl has the largest exploitability reduction, $79.1\%$.
The game-wise mean of $C_k^G$ ranges from $0.177$ to $0.587$.
With $\bS_k\triangleq\sym(\nabla\bF(\bz_k))$ and $\bW_k\triangleq\skewop(\nabla\bF(\bz_k))$, the median ratio $\norm{\bW_k\bF(\bz_k)}/(\norm{\bS_k\bF(\bz_k)}+10^{-15})$ is $0.951$ on FrequencyHopping and $0.966$ on RoutingInterdiction.
Thus the controller is not a hard skew-threshold switch: Theorem~\ref{thm:skew_dominance} concerns $+\bG$ alone, whereas the composite QP may use curvature through the gap gradient and cross-curvature even below the skew threshold.

\subsection{Finite-Trajectory Oracle Validation}\label{subsec:finite_trajectory}
The preceding population-oracle studies evaluate the controller with exact expectations and therefore isolate its deterministic geometry.
We now test whether the same two-direction mechanism remains observable when the training field, curvature action, and local-model stencil are all estimated from finitely many trajectories.
At each of $60$ CyclicControl updates, two $4$--$8$--$3$ policies use $128$ horizon-$16$ trajectories ($2048$ transitions).
One detached-policy batch $\mathcal B_k$ supplies every stencil through unclipped per-decision prefix ratios and the infinitely differentiable Monte Carlo estimator (DiCE) \cite{Foerster2018DiCE}.
DiCE gives the regularized field and Hessian--vector curvature oracles, with the field detached in the latter product.
For $\lambda_P=0$, an independent-batch-normalized field-energy merit controls training, while population quantities are used only for checkpoint evaluation.
Proposition~\ref{prop:model_approx} and Theorem~\ref{thm:ergodic} account for dependent same-batch stencil errors.

\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================
This paper developed complementary field and curvature directions for zero-sum Markov-game policy optimization.
The symmetric--antisymmetric analysis identifies when curvature decreases field energy, while a closed-form box QP selects independent step sizes from a second-order Lyapunov-drift model.
Under local geometric coverage and explicit stochastic-error budgets, the stationarity guarantee gives an $O(1/K)$ ergodic transient plus an error floor; with the local policy-space error bound, it transfers to Nash-gap and worst-case-return bounds.
Population-oracle and finite-trajectory experiments support the predicted geometry and show improved worst-case return and exploitability over the field-only ablation.
Independent step sizes thereby use curvature when its local contribution is beneficial without requiring it to be a universally descending direction.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:DTA}}\label{app:DTA}
The GDA claim is immediate from Eq.~\eqref{eq:classical_updates}.
On the closed ball $\bar B(\bz;r)\subset\cD$, let $M_i$ and $B_F$ bound $\nabla^i\bF$ ($i=1,2$) and $\bF$.
For $s\le\min\{r/B_F,(2M_1)^{-1},1\}$, the contraction $T_s(\bu)=\bz-s\bF(\bu)$ has PPM fixed-point displacement $\norm{\Delta}\le sB_F$.
Taylor expansion yields $\Delta+s\bF=-s\nabla\bF\,\Delta-s\mathbf r_2$, $\norm{\mathbf r_2}\le M_2\norm{\Delta}^2/2$, and hence $\Delta=-s\bF+s^2\nabla\bF\,\bF+O(s^3)$ uniformly.
For EGM, $\bF(\bz-s\bF)=\bF-s\nabla\bF\,\bF+O(s^2)$ uniformly; multiplication by $-s$ gives the same expansion.
\hfill$\blacksquare$

\paragraph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:decomp}}\label{app:decomp}
With $\bA=\nabla\bF$ and $\nabla\phi=\bA^\top\bF$, $\inner{\nabla\phi}{-\bF}=-\bF^\top\bS\bF$ and $\inner{\nabla\phi}{\bG}=\bF^\top\bA^2\bF$.
Expanding $\bA^2$ gives $\bS^2+\bS\bW+\bW\bS+\bW^2$; the cross term is antisymmetric, whereas $\bF^\top\bS^2\bF=\norm{\bS\bF}^2$ and $\bF^\top\bW^2\bF=-\norm{\bW\bF}^2$, proving Eq.~\eqref{eq:dphi_F} and Eq.~\eqref{eq:dphi_G}.
\hfill$\blacksquare$

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:skew_dominance}}\label{app:skew_dominance}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dphi_G} proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:skew_dominance}.
For (i), $\bS\bF=0$ gives $\inner{\nabla\phi}{\bG}=-\norm{\bW\bF}^{2}<0$ when $\bW\bF\ne0$, while Eq.~\eqref{eq:dphi_F} gives $\inner{\nabla\phi}{-\bF}=0$.
For (ii), $\bW=0$ gives $\inner{\nabla\phi}{\bG}=\norm{\bS\bF}^{2}\ge0$.
For (iii), normality on the invariant plane gives $\bS=\mu\Id$ and $\bW=\sigma J_0$; hence Eq.~\eqref{eq:skew_dominance} is equivalent to $\lvert\sigma\rvert>\lvert\mu\rvert$.
\hfill$\blacksquare$

\paragraph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:Lyap_properties}}\label{app:Lyap_properties}
Nonnegativity gives $\Vk\ge0$; $\Vk=0$ forces $\cE_F=0$ and thus $\bF=0$ because $\lambda_F>0$ and $\bW_V\succ0$.

\hfill$\blacksquare$

\paragraph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:drift_taylor}}\label{app:drift_taylor}

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:coverage_sufficient}}\label{app:coverage_sufficient}
For $\widetilde{\bA}(\bz)\triangleq\bW_V^{1/2}\bA(\bz)\bW_V^{-1/2}$, define $\widetilde{\bS}(\bz)\triangleq\sym(\widetilde{\bA}(\bz))$, $\widetilde{\bW}(\bz)\triangleq\skewop(\widetilde{\bA}(\bz))$, and
\[
h(\bz,\bu)\triangleq\max\!\left\{\bu^\top\widetilde{\bS}(\bz)\bu,\,\norm{\widetilde{\bW}(\bz)\bu}^2-\norm{\widetilde{\bS}(\bz)\bu}^2\right\}.
\]
At $\bz^\star$, a unit $\bu$ either has $\bu^\top\widetilde{\bS}(\bz^\star)\bu>0$, or positive semidefiniteness gives $\widetilde{\bS}(\bz^\star)\bu=0$ and nonsingularity gives $\norm{\widetilde{\bW}(\bz^\star)\bu}=\norm{\widetilde{\bA}_\star\bu}>0$.
Hence $h>0$ on the compact unit sphere, so continuity yields $c_\star>0$ and a neighborhood $\cN_\star$ where $h\ge c_\star$ uniformly.
For $\Phi(\bz)>0$, set $\bu=\bW_V^{1/2}\bF(\bz)/\sqrt{\Phi(\bz)}$; then $\norm{\bu}=1$, $\chi_F/\Phi=\bu^\top\widetilde{\bS}\bu$, and $\chi_G/\Phi=\norm{\widetilde{\bW}\bu}^{2}-\norm{\widetilde{\bS}\bu}^{2}$, which proves the result.
\hfill$\blacksquare$
